### QA-069 — Wealth vs Development: Terminology & Coherence

**Status:** ✅ Implemented

**Problem:** Users (including Neil Lucock) found that "Average Wealth + UnderDeveloped Development" felt contradictory. The "Average Wealth" description said "The working population meets basic needs with little surplus" while "UnderDeveloped" said "Subsistence economy dominates. High infant mortality, minimal infrastructure..." — these descriptions contradict when placed side by side.

**Root cause:** The label "Wealth" implies population prosperity, but the metric actually measures **material economic output** — natural resources, trade surplus, GDP. A world can be resource-rich (high Wealth) while its population is poor (low Development). Ghana is the prime example: incredible natural resources but underdeveloped institutions.

**Solution:**

1. **Renamed UI label:** "Material Wealth" → **"Resources"** across:
   - `InhabitantsDemographicsPanel.tsx`
   - `InhabitantsTab.tsx`
   - `OverviewTab.tsx`

2. **Expanded coherence notes** in `getWealthDevelopmentContext()` to catch close-rank gaps (-1, 0, +1), not just large gaps (±2+):

| Combination | Gap | Real-world analogue | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average + UnderDeveloped | 0 | Haiti, Malawi | Resource-scarce, underdeveloped. Population scrapes by on subsistence. |
| Better-off + UnderDeveloped | -1 | Equatorial Guinea, DRC | Resource-rich world with poor population. Value captured by elites/off-world powers. |
| Average + Developing | +1 | Bangladesh, 1990s Vietnam | Industrialising but struggling. Thin surpluses, most live close to subsistence. |
| Prosperous + Developing | -1 | China, Brazil | Booming economy outpacing institutions. Corruption and inequality side-effects. |
| Affluent + Mature | -1 | Qatar, Kuwait, Nauru | Extreme resource wealth in mature but not cutting-edge economy. |
| Prosperous + Developed | +1 | Spain, Italy, South Korea | Developed society with moderate resources. Steady, stable, broadly equitable. |

3. **Updated Glossary entry** for Resources with Ghana, Equatorial Guinea, Singapore, and Japan examples.
